### Consumer Grade Solid State Hard Drives
![[Pasted image 20261109164446.png]]

---
#### The Oldest One

Intel X25-M drives - 2008
![[Pasted image 20261019182733.png]]
Guys on forums and Reddit say theirs still works [1](https://www.reddit.com/r/buildapc/comments/1igs6kn/have_you_used_an_ssd_drive_to_the_limit_of_its/), [2](https://www.reddit.com/r/homelab/comments/uge51s/old_ssd_intel_x25m/), [3]([https://forum.level1techs.com/t/what-is-your-longest-running-ssd/236678?page=3](https://forum.level1techs.com/t/what-is-your-longest-running-ssd/236678?page=3) But there does not seem to be a centralized way of checking this; like a group of the drive users, that is not a thing; the manufacturing company is not doing this.

---
#### General Lifespan

If you plug it in *every year* it looks like around 20 years is possible. This is still emergent!

If you don't plug it in every year, it could die in one year. Or it could not. Maybe it's fine, maybe it's corrupted, maybe some files are corrupted, maybe it's even recoverable. It becomes like Schrodinger's Cat. Hard drives are complicated and that complication is *a core question of digital preservation*. 

TLDR: Check your hard drives!

## Takeaways
1. The older the format, the longer it lasts...*sorta*...
2. BUT this sample is biased because *those examples lasted.*
3. The new formats haven't lasted long enough for us to truly know how long they can last.
	1. IMO, this is what makes Digital Preservation fun. Questions about "Unknown futures." 
4. The community of people actually checking into this stuff is surprisingly small and  needs more workers and volunteers!
